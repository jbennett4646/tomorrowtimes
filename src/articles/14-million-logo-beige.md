Regional restaurant chain Hearthstone Kitchen unveiled its long-awaited new logo Tuesday, the result of a two-year, $14 million rebranding effort, revealing a design that customers, employees, and at least one ophthalmologist have confirmed is the old logo in a slightly different shade of beige.

“We’re thrilled to introduce the new Hearthstone,” said CEO Laura Pembrook, standing in front of a 30-foot banner displaying the logo to a silent room. “This is a bold step toward our future. A future that is warmer. More modern. And about four percent less tan.”

The previous logo, which had been in use since 1978, featured the words “Hearthstone Kitchen” in a rustic serif font on a beige background, beneath a hand-drawn illustration of a stone fireplace. The new logo features the words “hearthstone” in lowercase sans-serif letters on a slightly lighter beige background, without the fireplace.

“We found the fireplace was holding us back,” explained Chief Brand Officer Devin Saltz. “It was too specific. Too literal. It told people exactly what we were. We wanted a logo that could mean anything. Or nothing. We landed somewhere in between.”

According to company documents, the rebrand was led by Meridian & Vale, an international branding agency whose previous clients include two banks, a hospital network, and a vitamin company that all now share nearly identical logos.

The process included 18 months of research, 41 workshops, 212 color studies, and a 90-page report titled *Toward a More Neutral Hearth.*

“The color is called ‘Harmonious Oat,’” Saltz said. “It’s a warm but restrained neutral that communicates comfort without commitment. The old color was called ‘Beige.’ That’s all anyone called it. It had no aspirations.”

Customers expressed confusion when the logo was revealed on social media.

“I put the old logo and the new logo side by side and I honestly can’t tell which is which,” said regular customer Betty Harlan, 68. “My granddaughter says the new one is a little lighter. My husband says it’s the same. We had an argument about it at dinner. At Hearthstone. The food hasn’t changed at all. It’s still good. The fireplace is gone, though. From the logo. The real fireplace is still there. They didn’t touch that.”

Local optometrist Dr. Allen Morris said he conducted an informal test with patients.

“I showed both logos to 40 people,” Morris said. “Twenty-six couldn’t see a difference. Eleven said the new one looked ‘sad.’ Three asked if their eyes were okay. One cried.”

Branding experts say the Hearthstone rebrand follows a familiar pattern. “Companies spend years and millions trying to modernize their image,” said marketing professor Dr. Karen Lin. “And they almost always land on the same result: less personality, fewer details, lighter colors, and a font that looks like a tech startup’s terms of service. It’s not a rebrand. It’s a sedative.”

When asked whether the $14 million could have been spent elsewhere, such as lowering menu prices or raising employee wages, Pembrook said the question “misunderstands the value of brand equity.”

“Our customers may not notice the difference today,” she said. “But over time, they’ll feel it.”

At press time, Hearthstone had announced a second phase of the rebrand, in which the beige will be made “roughly two percent more hopeful.”
