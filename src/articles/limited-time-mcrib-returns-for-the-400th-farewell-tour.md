McDonald's announced Monday that the McRib will return to menus nationwide for a "final, final, truly final, we mean it this time" limited run, marking the sandwich's 400th farewell tour and its third one this calendar year.

"This is it," said a visibly exhausted brand spokesperson, Greg Tollefsen, reading from a card that had been laminated for reuse. "The McRib is going away forever. Enjoy it while you can. Tell your loved ones. This is goodbye."

Tollefsen then flipped the card over, revealing the words "Welcome Back!" in the same font, and said, "Sorry, wrong side."

According to internal documents, the McRib has never actually been discontinued. Instead, the sandwich lives full-time in a climate-controlled vault beneath corporate headquarters, where it is kept in a state of suspended anticipation until quarterly earnings require a bump.

"People think the scarcity is about supply," said one former marketing employee, who asked to remain anonymous because the vault "can hear." "It's not. We have millions of them. Enough to feed the nation through a nuclear winter. The scarcity is emotional. The McRib doesn't leave because it's rare. It leaves so you'll miss it."

The employee described a department of 40 people whose sole job is to manage the McRib's public absence. Duties include drafting fake leaked memos about its return, planting rumors on social media, and occasionally announcing a "McRib Locator" app that only works while the sandwich is unavailable.

Customers say the cycle has taken its toll.

"I've said goodbye to the McRib more times than I've said goodbye to my father," said Luis Carrera, 44, of Columbus, Ohio, standing in a line of 30 cars. "The last time they announced it was leaving forever, I drove four hours to have one more. Then it came back six weeks later. I felt used. I also got two."

Carrera said he now experiences a Pavlovian grief response whenever he sees the color brown.

The company says this farewell tour will include new "commemorative" packaging, a limited-edition McRib-scented candle, and a documentary titled *McRib: The Last Ride (Part 11)*. Industry analysts expect the sandwich to disappear again in November, reappear in February, and announce its retirement in April at a press conference where it will be shown to be clearly still in the building.

At press time, McDonald's had confirmed that the McRib would "absolutely never return," prompting a 12% jump in pork futures.
