dateline: "CANTON, GA"

Local husband Ryan Mercer, 37, was informed Saturday evening that his wife's disappointment over his failure to buy her an anniversary gift was valid, while his own disappointment over receiving no gift from her was "not eligible for consideration."

"I asked her what she wanted at least six times," Mercer said. "Two weeks before. One week before. Three days before. The night before. Each time she said, 'Nothing, I'm good, don't worry about it.' Once she said, 'Surprise me.' Once she said, 'You know what I like.' I don't. That's why I was asking."

On the morning of their anniversary, Mercer said he made coffee, wished his wife a happy anniversary, and handled breakfast for their son while she slept in. That evening, his wife expressed disappointment that he "didn't do anything."

"I said, 'You told me not to get anything,'" Mercer said. "She said, 'I didn't mean it literally.' I asked how I was supposed to know which statements were literal. She said I should have known."

Mercer then pointed out, carefully, that she had not given him a gift or card either.

"That's when things got technical," he said. "Apparently, there's a rule. Her not getting me anything was because she was busy. Me not getting her anything was because I didn't care. Same action, different motive, determined entirely by her."

Relationship researchers say the phenomenon, known as Asymmetric Disappointment Eligibility, is common in long-term marriages.

"In many households, only one spouse's disappointment is officially recognized," said Dr. Paul Hendricks, who studies marital dynamics. "The other spouse's disappointment is classified as 'deflection,' 'keeping score,' or 'making this about you.' It's a closed system."

Mercer said he has accepted the ruling but requested clarification for next year.

"I just want the rules in writing," he said. "If 'nothing' means 'something,' what does 'something' mean? 'Everything'? I need a glossary."

At press time, Mercer's wife had told him she was "fine," a word he is now studying with a translator.
