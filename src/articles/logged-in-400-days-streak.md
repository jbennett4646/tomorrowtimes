Local man Tyler Brennan, 27, unlocked the rare "Unbroken" achievement in *Combat Duty 27* on Sunday, marking 400 consecutive days of logging into a video game he says he stopped enjoying "sometime during the first weekend."

"Day one was fun," Brennan said, holding his phone up to show the animated gold badge, which spins slowly while a choir sings. "Day two was okay. Day three, I remember thinking, 'This is kind of the same as day two.' Then I got a pop-up saying I was on a three-day streak, and I thought, well, I can't stop now. That was 397 days ago."

Brennan's streak survived a bout of the flu, two power outages, his grandmother's funeral, and his own wedding, where he excused himself between the vows and the reception to log in from a church bathroom.

"My wife was very understanding," Brennan said. "She's on a 212-day streak in a farming game. She doesn't even like farming. We support each other."

According to Brennan, his daily routine now consists of opening the game, collecting a login reward, spinning a prize wheel, claiming his battle pass progress, dismissing a limited-time bundle offer, and closing the game. The process takes about four minutes, and he has not played an actual match in seven months.

"I don't really play anymore," he said. "I just maintain it. It's like a lawn."

Brennan said he has tried to quit several times, but each attempt was met with a notification reading, "Tyler, your squad misses you," featuring cartoon characters with tears in their eyes.

"I don't have a squad," he said. "I've never played with anyone. They're just drawings. But the drawings are crying."

Experts say Brennan's experience is typical. "Modern games are designed around the sunk cost fallacy," said behavioral economist Dr. Rebecca Choi. "The longer the streak, the harder it is to walk away, because breaking it means admitting all those days meant nothing. Which they did. That's the business model."

Vanta Interactive, the game's publisher, celebrated Brennan's achievement in a statement, calling him "the kind of dedicated fan who makes all of this worthwhile."

"Tyler represents everything we value in a player," the statement read. "Consistency. Commitment. A complete absence of joy that does not affect his willingness to log in. We could not be prouder."

Brennan said the badge came with a reward: a weapon skin that he will never use, since he does not play matches.

"It's a gold shotgun," he said. "It's beautiful. I looked at it for a while."

At press time, Brennan had received a notification informing him that the game's servers would be permanently shut down next month to make way for *Combat Duty 28*, and that streaks would not carry over.
