Despite a petition signed by more than 2 million viewers pleading with executives to "please, for the love of God, let her rest," the Keystone Network announced Thursday that it will revive the drama *Hollow Creek* for a fourth time.

"We've heard the fans loud and clear," said Keystone's Senior Vice President of Legacy Programming, Doug Renner. "They're passionate. They care deeply about this show. And that passion tells us there's still an audience for it, even if that audience is specifically asking us to stop."

The petition, titled *Let Hollow Creek Die With Dignity*, launched last month after rumors surfaced of yet another revival. It currently has 2.3 million signatures and includes more than 400,000 comments, most of them variations on "Why are you doing this to us?"

"I loved *Hollow Creek*," wrote one signer. "The first run was perfect. Four seasons. Clear ending. Everyone got closure. Then they brought it back in 2015 and the sheriff was suddenly a werewolf. Then they brought it back in 2020 and it was set in space. Then in 2023 it was a musical. Please. We just want to remember it the way it was."

*Hollow Creek* originally aired from 2004 to 2008 and concluded with a widely praised finale in which the main character, Sheriff Nora Whitfield, rode off into the sunset. The show has since been revived three times, bringing Nora back from retirement, from a coma, and in the most recent revival, from the dead.

"She died in 2020," Renner confirmed. "But in 2023, we revealed it was a clone. And in the new revival, we'll reveal the clone had a clone. So technically, we have two Noras now. Maybe three. We're still working out the details."

The new revival, *Hollow Creek: Ascension*, will reportedly feature Nora and her clones returning to their hometown to investigate a mystery that "connects every previous version of the show." Writers have promised the series will "finally answer the questions fans have been asking," though fans have noted the only question they've been asking is "Why won't you let it go?"

The show's original star, Diane Walsh, 64, said she agreed to return "reluctantly."

"I retired," Walsh said. "I moved to Vermont. I got chickens. I was happy. Then they called me and said, 'Diane, the fans need you.' I said the fans literally signed a petition asking you not to do this. They said, 'That's engagement.'"

Television historians say *Hollow Creek* is a textbook example of what they call "necromantic programming."

"Networks used to cancel shows and move on," said media scholar Dr. Owen Reyes. "Now nothing is ever allowed to stay dead. Shows are exhumed, reanimated, and paraded around until the audience is too exhausted to fight back. The fans aren't asking for a revival. They're asking for a funeral."

Renner insisted the new series will be "the final chapter," adding that the network has also greenlit a spinoff following Nora's clone's clone.

At press time, the petition had been updated with a new request: that the network at least let the chickens stay in Vermont.
