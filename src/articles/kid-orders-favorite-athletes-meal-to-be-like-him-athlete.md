Ten-year-old Houston resident Mason Rivera ordered the "Deshawn Carter Power Meal" at a BurgerFort drive-thru Saturday afternoon, telling his mother he wanted to "eat like Deshawn so I can be fast like him," at approximately the same moment his hero, star NFL running back Deshawn Carter, was sitting down to a lunch of kale salad, grilled branzino, and a glass of filtered water at a private training facility 900 miles away.

"He's my favorite player," Mason said, holding the Power Meal bag, which features a picture of Carter mid-sprint. "He's the fastest guy in the league. If I eat what he eats, I'll be fast too."

The Deshawn Carter Power Meal includes a triple-patty cheeseburger, a large order of fries topped with cheese sauce and bacon bits, a 40-ounce orange soda, and four mini chocolate donuts called "Touchdown Bites." The bag claims the meal is "Deshawn's go-to order."

"He's so excited," said Mason's mother, Elena Rivera. "He has a Deshawn poster on his wall. He wears his jersey to school every Friday. When the meal came out, he asked me every day if we could get it. I finally said yes."

Carter, reached for comment through his publicist, confirmed that he has never eaten the Power Meal.

"Deshawn follows a very strict, performance-based nutrition plan," the publicist said. "His typical lunch consists of leafy greens, lean protein, and complex carbohydrates. He does not consume fast food during the season, or the off-season, or really ever."

According to Carter's personal chef, the running back's actual favorite meal is "seared salmon with roasted vegetables and a quinoa salad," which he eats four times a week.

"He loves quinoa," the chef said. "He calls it 'the seed of greatness.' He's never had cheese sauce. I don't even think he knows where BurgerFort is."

Sports dietitians say the gap between athletes' real diets and their signature meals sends a confusing message to young fans. "Kids idolize these athletes," said Dr. Rachel Moore, a pediatric nutritionist. "They want to do what their heroes do. When an athlete puts his name on a 2,500-calorie meal, kids believe that's what champions eat. Meanwhile, the champion is eating kale."

Mason, meanwhile, reported mixed results after finishing the meal.

"I ate the whole thing," he said, lying on the living room couch. "I tried to run around the backyard afterward. I made it to the trampoline and had to lie down. I don't think I'm faster. I think I'm slower. Maybe Deshawn's body is just different."

At press time, Mason's mother had shown him a social media video of Carter eating a kale salad, prompting Mason to declare that he would begin eating "the seed of greatness," before learning that BurgerFort does not sell quinoa.
