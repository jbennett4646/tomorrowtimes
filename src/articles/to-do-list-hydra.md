---
title: "Man Crosses One Item Off Wife’s To-Do List, Three New Items Grow in Its Place"
section: home-front
date: 2026-10-28
dateline: "MARIETTA, GA"
---

Marietta resident Scott Halvorsen, 43, stood in stunned silence in his kitchen Saturday morning after crossing “fix squeaky cabinet” off his wife’s to-do list and watching three new items appear in its place almost immediately.

“I took the pen, drew a line through it, felt good for maybe four seconds,” Halvorsen said. “Then my wife walked by, glanced at the list, and said, ‘Oh, since you’ve got the screwdriver out,’ and wrote ‘tighten the bathroom towel bar,’ ‘look at the closet door,’ and ‘the thing in the garage.’”

Biologists at Emory University say the phenomenon, which they have dubbed Domestic Hydra Syndrome, appears in roughly 94% of American households.

“In Greek mythology, when Hercules cut off one of the Hydra’s heads, two more grew back,” said Dr. Rachel Moreno, who studies household task ecology. “The modern to-do list follows a similar biology, except it’s more aggressive. For every item removed, an average of 2.7 new items emerge. The list is not shrinking. It’s evolving.”

Moreno’s research team tracked 300 couples over six months and found that no participating household ever reduced its to-do list below its starting length. One list in Ohio grew from 12 items to 186 despite the husband completing 71 tasks during the study.

“He was the most productive man we’ve ever observed,” Moreno said. “And he ended the study further behind than when he started.”

Halvorsen’s wife, Melissa, said she doesn’t see the issue.

“I’m not adding things to be mean,” she said. “When he fixes one thing, it reminds me of other things. It’s like when you clean one corner of a room, and suddenly the rest of the room looks dirty. That’s not my fault. That’s the room’s fault.”

Halvorsen said he has considered hiding his completed tasks.

“I thought, what if I just fix things and don’t cross them off?” he said. “But she’d find out. She always finds out. She’s like a detective. She noticed I tightened a doorknob once from the other side of the house.”

At press time, Halvorsen had crossed off “tighten the bathroom towel bar,” prompting his wife to add “re-caulk the tub,” “replace the shower head,” and “we should really redo the whole bathroom at some point.”
