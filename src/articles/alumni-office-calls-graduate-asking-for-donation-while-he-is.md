Ridgemont State University graduate Brandon Torres, 22, received a call from the university's alumni giving office Saturday morning while he was still walking off the commencement stage, diploma in hand, asking whether he would consider "giving back to the institution that made him who he is."

"I shook the dean's hand, got my diploma, turned around to walk down the steps, and my phone started buzzing in my gown pocket," Torres said. "I thought it was my mom saying congratulations. It was a student caller from the alumni office. She said, 'Hi, Brandon! Congratulations on graduating! As a new member of the Ridgemont alumni family, would you like to make your first gift today?'"

Torres said he was still on the stage stairs when the call began.

"I literally hadn't even sat down yet," he said. "I was holding my diploma in one hand and my phone in the other. My family was screaming my name from the stands. And this girl is asking if I'd like to start with $50 or $100."

Torres said he politely declined, explaining that he graduated with $142,000 in student loans and does not yet have a job.

"She said, 'I completely understand! A lot of our alumni start small. Even $10 makes a difference!'" Torres said. "I told her I'd think about it. She said she'd mark me down as a 'maybe' and that someone would follow up next week."

According to Torres, by the time he returned to his seat, he had already received two emails from the alumni office, one welcoming him to the alumni association and one inviting him to join the "Young Alumni Giving Circle." By the time the ceremony ended, a fundraising mailer had been placed on the windshield of his parents' car in the parking lot.

"There was a brochure under the wiper," said Torres' father, Michael. "It said, 'Your Ridgemont Story Is Just Beginning. Help Write the Next Chapter.' There was a return envelope. They'd even pre-filled my son's name."

Ridgemont's Director of Alumni Engagement, Sarah Collins, defended the timing.

"Commencement is an emotional moment," she said. "Graduates feel a deep sense of gratitude and connection to the university. We believe that's the perfect time to give them an opportunity to express that gratitude financially."

When asked whether graduates might feel overwhelmed by fundraising requests on the day they receive their diplomas, Collins said the university has data showing that "donor intent peaks at commencement and declines sharply once the student loan bills arrive."

At press time, Ridgemont had announced plans to begin alumni fundraising calls at freshman orientation, so students can "start their giving journey early."
