Microsoft unveiled a new artificial intelligence feature for Windows 11 on Thursday, designed to help users locate settings, options, and controls that the company itself has moved, renamed, or hidden over the past several years.

"Windows is more powerful than ever," said Microsoft Product Director Allison Grant at a launch event. "But we know that sometimes users have trouble finding certain features. That's why we've developed an intelligent assistant that can help users navigate Windows, including all the places we've moved things."

The feature, called Copilot Settings Assistant, allows users to describe what they're looking for in plain English. The AI then guides them through the menus, explaining where the setting used to be, where it is now, and "why it's better there."

"For example, many users remember that the Control Panel used to be easy to find," Grant said. "Now, many of those options have moved to Settings. Some are in Settings, then in a submenu, then in a pop-up window, then in the old Control Panel, which still exists but is harder to find. Our AI knows exactly where all of it is."

During the demonstration, Grant asked the assistant, "How do I change my default browser?" The AI responded with a 14-step walkthrough, including clicking Settings, clicking Apps, clicking Default Apps, scrolling, selecting Microsoft Edge, and declining three separate prompts asking whether the user was sure.

"See?" Grant said. "Easy."

When an audience member asked why Microsoft didn't simply put settings back where they used to be, Grant smiled.

"That would require us to move them again," she said. "And we don't want to confuse users."

Early testers report that the assistant is helpful but occasionally editorializes. "I asked it how to remove the weather widget," said tester Mark Collins. "It told me how, but first it said, 'Are you sure? The weather widget helps you stay informed about the world around you.'"

Another tester said the assistant initially refused to help him turn off Start menu recommendations, instead offering to "personalize them to better match your interests."

IT professionals greeted the announcement with weary amusement. "Microsoft built a maze, and now it's selling us a map," said corporate IT director Lisa Monroe. "Next year, they'll move the map."

At press time, Microsoft had confirmed that the Copilot Settings Assistant button would be located in the taskbar, unless users had customized their taskbar, in which case it would be in the Start menu, unless Start menu recommendations were disabled, in which case users could ask the assistant where to find the assistant.
