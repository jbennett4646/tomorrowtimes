---
title: "Private Equity Firm Acquires Beloved Family Diner, Immediately Identifies Grandma as “Key Cost Center”"
section: private-equity
date: 2026-10-31
dateline: "DAHLONEGA, GA"
---

Just 48 hours after closing its acquisition of Mae’s Kitchen, a 52-year-old family diner beloved across north Georgia, private equity firm Ridgeline Capital Partners delivered its first operational assessment to staff, identifying 81-year-old founder Mae Whitlow as the restaurant’s “single largest cost center.”

“Mae is a tremendous brand asset,” said Ridgeline Operating Partner Chase Donnelly, reviewing a 64-page slide deck titled *Project Biscuit: Value Creation Plan.* “But from an efficiency standpoint, she’s a problem. She arrives at 4 a.m. to make biscuits by hand. She uses real butter. She knows every customer’s name and talks to them. That’s not scalable.”

According to the deck, Mae’s labor-intensive practices account for an estimated 11% of the diner’s operating costs, including hand-rolled biscuits, from-scratch gravy, pies baked daily from a recipe her mother brought from Tennessee in 1947, and what analysts flagged as “excessive customer conversation time.”

“She spent 14 minutes talking to a widower about his tomatoes last Tuesday,” Donnelly said. “Fourteen minutes. At her effective hourly rate, that conversation cost us $6.40, and the man only ordered coffee.”

Ridgeline’s plan, which it describes as “respectful modernization,” includes replacing Mae’s biscuits with a frozen product from a supplier in Ohio, swapping her pie recipe for a pre-portioned dessert SKU, and moving Mae herself into a new role as “Heritage Ambassador,” which consists primarily of appearing in a framed photograph near the register.

“We’re honoring her legacy,” Donnelly said. “Her face will be on every menu. Her story will be on the placemats. We just won’t have her in the building. That’s the best of both worlds.”

Longtime customers were not persuaded.

“The biscuits are why people come here,” said Earl Hutchins, 77, who has eaten breakfast at Mae’s nearly every morning since 1981. “I watched her make them for 40 years. She’d flour her hands and hum old hymns. You could taste it. Now they’re telling me the biscuits are coming from Ohio in a box? I’ve been to Ohio. I wasn’t impressed.”

Ridgeline acquired the diner at 5.5x EBITDA using roughly 70% debt, which the firm placed on the diner’s own balance sheet. The deck projects that cost cuts, price increases, and “menu simplification” will raise EBITDA 40% in three years, allowing Ridgeline to sell the business at 9x to a larger restaurant platform.

“Our thesis is simple,” Donnelly said. “Mae’s Kitchen has incredible goodwill. People drive two hours for this food. We’re going to harvest that goodwill very efficiently.”

When asked what happens after the goodwill is fully harvested, Donnelly checked his watch and said Ridgeline’s hold period is five years.

Mae Whitlow, reached at her home, said she was “still thinking it over.”

“My grandson said selling was the smart thing to do,” she said. “Said they’d keep everything the same. Then a young man in a vest came in and timed me rolling biscuits with a stopwatch. I’ve been timed before. By the Lord. He never complained.”

At press time, Ridgeline had announced that the diner’s famous hand-painted sign reading *Everybody’s Welcome at Mae’s* would be replaced with a digital menu board reading *Order at Kiosk.*
