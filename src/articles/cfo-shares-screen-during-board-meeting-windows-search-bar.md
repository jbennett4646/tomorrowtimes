The quarterly board meeting of Hollister Industrial Holdings took an unexpected turn Tuesday when Chief Financial Officer Margaret Collins shared her screen to present the company's $40 million debt refinancing plan, and directors were greeted by a cartoon turkey dancing in the Windows search bar beneath the words "Happy National Gravy Day!"

"I clicked 'Share Screen,' opened my deck, and immediately heard someone on the call say, 'Is that a turkey?'" Collins said. "I looked down and there it was. A little animated turkey in a pilgrim hat, doing a jig next to a gravy boat. I had no idea it was there. I've never asked Windows for a turkey."

According to meeting participants, the turkey remained visible for the entire 45-minute presentation, positioned directly beneath slides covering interest rate exposure, covenant compliance, and the company's liquidity runway.

"Every time she talked about leverage ratios, the turkey would sort of bob up and down," said board member Richard Ames. "When she said, 'We may face significant headwinds in Q3,' the turkey ladled gravy onto a plate. It was very hard to focus."

Collins said she attempted to hide the turkey by resizing her window, but that the search bar remained visible at the bottom of the screen. At one point, she clicked the search bar to make it disappear, which opened a full-screen Bing panel titled *Fun Facts About Gravy* alongside trending searches including "best Black Friday TV deals" and "can dogs eat stuffing."

"That was the low point," she said. "The entire board of directors watched me close a pop-up about whether dogs can eat stuffing while I was explaining our refinancing strategy."

The turkey, part of a Windows feature Microsoft calls "Search Highlights," displays rotating illustrations, holidays, trivia, and news in the taskbar search box. The feature is enabled by default.

"We believe Search Highlights brings moments of joy and discovery to the Windows experience," said a Microsoft spokesperson in a statement provided for this story. "Our users love learning about holidays they didn't know existed."

When asked how many users had requested a dancing turkey during professional presentations, the spokesperson said the company "doesn't break out turkey-specific engagement metrics."

Corporate IT professionals say the incident is not unique. "We've had executives present with pumpkins, snowmen, a cartoon groundhog, and once a celebration of National Pickle Day," said IT director Kevin Marsh. "One time our general counsel was walking the board through litigation risk, and the search bar was celebrating 'Squirrel Appreciation Day.'"

At press time, the Hollister board had approved the refinancing plan and passed a separate resolution requiring all company computers to disable Search Highlights, which IT reported could be done in "only 11 steps."
