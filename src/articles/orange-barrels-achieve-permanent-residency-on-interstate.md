A group of approximately 1,400 orange construction barrels lining a 12-mile stretch of Interstate 40 celebrated their 10th anniversary Saturday, marking a decade since they were first placed on the highway for what the state Department of Transportation originally described as "a short-term resurfacing project lasting approximately six months."

"We came here in 2016 thinking it'd be a quick job," said one barrel, through a spokesperson for the DOT. "Maybe a summer. We didn't bring much. Just our reflective stripes and a sandbag. Now we've been here so long, we've got roots."

The barrels were placed along I-40 in April 2016 to support a lane-resurfacing project. Construction was paused in 2017 for funding review, resumed in 2018, paused again in 2019 for a design change, restarted in 2021, and paused again in 2023 "pending reevaluation of the reevaluation."

Throughout these delays, the barrels remained in place.

"At this point, they're part of the landscape," said DOT spokesperson Megan Lyle. "They've outlasted two governors, four project managers, and a Waffle House that closed and reopened as a different Waffle House."

Commuters say they've developed emotional attachments to the barrels. "I pass them every morning," said Franklin resident Dave Morris. "There's one near Exit 207 with a dent in it shaped like Florida. I call him Carl. I wave to Carl. When it snows, I worry about Carl."

The barrels were officially granted "permanent residency" status last month after the state determined that removing them would require a new environmental review, since the barrels have become "a long-standing feature of the transportation corridor."

To mark the anniversary, the DOT hosted a small ceremony at a rest stop, featuring a cake decorated with orange frosting stripes and a banner reading *10 Years of Temporary.*

"These barrels have been here longer than most leases," Lyle said. "Some of them are older than the cars driving past them. They've seen things. Thousands of near misses. Three proposals. One guy who changed a flat tire while singing Garth Brooks."

At press time, the DOT had announced the project would resume in 2027, with officials assuring drivers that the barrels "won't be there much longer," a statement the barrels reportedly met with silence.
