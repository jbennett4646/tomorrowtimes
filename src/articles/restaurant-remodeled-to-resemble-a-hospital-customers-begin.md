Customers at a newly remodeled BurgerFort location in Macon have begun lining up at the ordering kiosks with insurance cards in hand, after the restaurant's corporate-mandated renovation left it virtually indistinguishable from a regional medical center.

"I walked in and a lady asked me what my symptoms were," said franchisee Rick Tolliver, 54, who spent $1.2 million on the upgrade. "She meant her symptoms. She wanted to know if she should see the triage nurse. I said, 'Ma'am, we have a two-for-five deal on chicken sandwiches.' She said, 'Is that covered?'"

The renovation, part of BurgerFort's company-wide ForwardFort modernization program, replaced the store's warm booths, wood paneling, and hand-painted mural of a smiling hamburger with white walls, white floors, white tables, and what corporate describes as "calming clinical lighting." Twelve touchscreen kiosks now greet customers in the lobby, arranged in a semicircle around a digital board displaying order numbers in the same font as an emergency room waiting list.

"The design team told us they wanted a 'clean, frictionless environment,'" Tolliver said. "They got it. There's no friction. There's also no warmth, no personality, and no sign that a human being has ever been happy in here."

Corporate guidelines required Tolliver to remove the front counter entirely, replacing it with a sealed pickup window labeled *Order Release Zone*. Crew members, now referred to as "Guest Fulfillment Specialists," wear pale blue uniforms that customers have repeatedly mistaken for scrubs.

"A man handed one of my cooks a urine sample," Tolliver said. "She's 17. She didn't know what to do. She gave him a milkshake."

The restaurant's ice cream machine has been placed in a glass enclosure beside a sign that reads *Currently Receiving Maintenance*. Regulars say the machine now appears to be on life support. "There's a little monitor next to it that beeps," said longtime customer Loretta Banks, 71. "Every once in a while, a technician comes in and checks its vitals. Nobody's allowed to visit it."

BurgerFort corporate praised the Macon remodel as "a flagship example of the future of dining."

"Our research shows that guests want speed, cleanliness, and consistency," said BurgerFort's Senior Vice President of Restaurant Experience, Jordan Pell. "A hospital is the most consistent environment humans have ever built. Nobody goes to a hospital looking for surprises. We think that same mindset works beautifully for a Quarter Pounder."

When asked about customer complaints that the new design feels sterile and impersonal, Pell said corporate is exploring solutions, including a soothing ambient soundtrack of gentle beeping and a digital greeter that asks guests to rate their hunger on a scale of 1 to 10.

Tolliver said business has changed since the remodel. "Families used to come here after Little League," he said. "Now I get people coming in after their colonoscopies because it feels familiar."

At press time, a customer had fainted near the drink station, and three other customers had calmly taken numbers and waited for the kiosk to call a doctor.
